This semester of ICS414 was a good extension of the foundational skills I first developed in ICS 314. While ICS 314 introduced me to the core principles of software development, this class pushed me to apply those principles in a more realistic, complex, and collaborative environment. It was not just about writing functional code anymore, but about designing systems, communicating ideas clearly, and working effectively as part of a team. This made the experience both challenging and rewarding.

The group project was the main assignment throughout the entire semester. Collaboration in a team setting turned out to be about as difficult as I expected. Coordinating schedules, aligning on design decisions, and ensuring that everyone stayed on the same page required consistent communication and effort. There were moments of friction and confusion, but I think those moments ultimately became opportunities to improve how we worked together. I definitely learned that strong communication is just as important as technical skill in software engineering. Being able to clearly explain ideas, ask for help, and adapt to others’ working styles was essential to making progress in the project.

Our project, the Shelf Awareness app for tracking pantry items, was a particularly intense and valuable learning experience. Through this project, I was able to develop both new technical skills and strengthen existing ones. For example, while working on the map features, I gained a much deeper understanding of how to integrate and use APIs within a React application. This was something I had only limited exposure to before, and applying it in a real project helped solidify that knowledge in a practical way.

Another major area of growth for me was deployment. Prior to this course, I had never deployed a full web application, so being responsible for handling the deployment of our project was both challenging and exciting. Learning how to take an application from development to a live environment gave me insight into an important part of the software development process that is often overlooked in smaller assignments.

Additionally, working with the database again after about two years away from software engineering required a significant amount of effort. I definitely had to spend time relearning concepts and thinking carefully about how to structure and manage our data. This process involved a lot of planning and problem-solving, but it ultimately strengthened my understanding of backend systems and data management.

Overall, I found this semester to be an enjoyable and meaningful experience. It challenged me in ways that went beyond coding and helped me grow as both a developer and a collaborator.
